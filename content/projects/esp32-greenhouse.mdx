## Strange Idea

What if a one-meter tower garden — printable, bucket-based, ESP32-driven — could grow food indoors for under twenty dollars of parts?

## Hypothesis

If the tower is modular and 3D-printable, the electronics are commodity ESP32, and the firmware flashes from a browser without touching code, then automated hydroponics stops being a kit purchase and becomes a weekend build.

## Constraints

- basic-build cost target under $20
- approachability for non-coders (flash from the docs site, no IDE)
- real growing: light, water, nutrients, airflow

## System

Commercial grow towers price out the people most interested in building them. This is an open-source modular tower garden: interlocking 3D-printable segments on a 20-liter bucket adapter, with an optional aeroponics high-pressure nozzle variant. An ESP32 runs lighting, pumps, fans, and sensors; firmware flashes over WebSerial straight from the browser, and the tower reports into Home Assistant over MQTT. Power and sensing ride a custom PCB with off-the-shelf fallbacks.

## Reality

Active DIY project with video demos and a kitted roadmap; the PCB is in prototype phase and the docs site is still being finished.

## Decisions

- Firmware moved from MicroPython to Arduino Core for more sensors, faster loops, and lower power.
- Website flashing over user-downloaded code, so non-coders can build one.

## Failure

- Scope keeps sprawling across tower, PCB, firmware, and docs; the PCB and docs lag the tower itself.

## Open Questions

- PCB files are still prototype — does the custom board ship or do off-the-shelf fallbacks become the real build?
- Docs site and WebSerial flashing are unfinished — what stays manual until they land?
- Arduino Core is now the main codebase with MicroPython legacy beside it — does the MicroPython path get dropped?
- MQTT discovery assumes one broker on the LAN — what breaks in multi-broker or no-mDNS homes?
